# SafelightIO — single-page site

Open `safelightio.html` in a browser. Images live in `assets/` and are referenced relatively. The header and the footer both offer the typeset manual (`assets/SafelightIO-User-Manual.pdf`); `MANUAL.md` remains the source text.

## Design rationale

**Sell the reading, not the software.** The page opens with the instrument at the easel, then immediately works a real manual example: 2.84 lux at Zone VI → 1.42 lux at Zone V → grade 3 at 2.5× → 23.3 s. That plate is the argument in miniature, and it gives the headline ("Measure the print before you make it") something concrete to stand on.

**A darkroom document, not a SaaS landing page.** Warm paper surfaces, near-black ink, a single terracotta accent, an editorial serif for headlines, and monospace for every number. The tools chapter flips to dark, the way the room does when printing starts. No gradients-as-decoration, no stock photography, no testimonial theatre.

**Evidence in the order a printer asks for it.** Five tools → craft and trust → "will it fit my darkroom?" → calibration → specifications → objections. Compatibility is a ledger, not a logo wall, and the paper list opens on demand. Fixed-grade behaviour and derived filter data are stated plainly, including the `~` convention.

**Conversion without clutter.** Price and VAT terms appear in the hero and again at the buy block; every primary CTA goes to the exact Shopify cart. The trust line is factual: handbuilt to order, 14-day returns, ships EU/US/CA/AU, direct support.

**The wedge is the instrument.** Between the hero and the tools sits a live eleven-step wedge — actual paper tones from blocked black to bare base, with a bracket marking the Zones II–VIII where texture survives. A probe can be dragged across it, tapped, or stepped with the arrow keys; the readout below prints the zone, its distance from Zone V, and the exposure multiple (Zone V: 0 stops, ×1.00; Zone VII: +2 stops, ×4.00). It is an ARIA radiogroup, so the same control works on a phone and at a keyboard, and the needle is the page's only accent in that viewport. The idea was taken from the current Cyanowood page's zone strip and rebuilt: hard-edged steps, a printable-range bracket, a physical probe, and copy that explains the system instead of decorating around it.

**The room has a safelight.** A small wall fixture with a pull cord hangs in the bottom-left corner. Pull it and the page goes darkroom-dark: paper becomes shadow, photographs take the lamp's tint, and the accent turns into the bulb. It is a real toggle — persisted, keyboard-operable, and reduced-motion aware — and every surface, border, and text token is re-derived in the safelight palette rather than simply inverted. The footer mentions it; the cord is the reward.

**One test strip, five tools.** Instead of five stacked articles, the chapter opens on a test strip: five exposed patches running paper-white to near-black, the density order a printer reads at the tray. Each patch is stamped with the tool's name and the number that tool returns — 2.84 lux, ΔEV 3.68, 36.4 s, a 40×30 grid, factor 2.50 — so the whole instrument is legible in one glance, before any scrolling. The patches are ARIA tabs: click, tap, or step with the arrow keys and one tool is exposed at a time, the accent needle sliding to the chosen patch while its plate develops below in a short blur-to-sharp pass and the patch takes a safelight underline. Nothing autoplays; the reader chooses the exposure. The chapter costs a fraction of its former scroll while the strip still holds all five capabilities. On phones the strip becomes a horizontal scroll-snap rail and the chosen patch is centred as it changes.

**Teach the workflow, not the chrome.** The tool figures are not screenshots or invented device mockups. Each is a dark instrument plate cut into the chapter — same near-black base, hairline grid, and mono labels — with a graphic built for its own calculation rather than one repeated skeleton: a two-stop chain over a Zone V track; a shadow-to-highlight ramp with a graded scale; a split bar that encodes the soft/hard share of light; a live easel grid with clipping bands, a Zone V column, and a ten-zone histogram; and an override scale comparing built-in and measured factors. Numbered steps follow, ending in the value the plate returns. The diagrams are kept deliberately shallow — one glance each — so a single panel reads without a scroll while the detail lives in the steps. The plates were rebuilt from the manual's tool sections, so the sequence matches what a printer does at the easel — and their palette is derived from the chapter's own ink, so they hold together when the safelight goes on. They also reflow to a single column at phone width, where a 1800 px screen photograph would not.

**The instrument, moving.** Four full-length portrait clips sit on the dark chapter's ink, framed at 9:16 with a poster frame and a single play control, so the section reads as evidence rather than a video wall. The clips are shown whole — no trimming — and only one plays at a time. A counter and a pair of hairline arrows in the ledger bar above the row step through them; the row is a scroll-snap track at every width (three across on desktop, two at tablet, a peeking single column on phones), so more clips can be appended without touching the layout. The footage was supplied with the project; nothing was staged or re-shot. All four slots currently use the same supplied session clip (`assets/reel-01.mp4`–`reel-04.mp4`); replacing those files is all that's needed to show distinct footage.

**The bench as a pile of prints.** Craft is shown the way a printer keeps evidence: seven supplied photographs in a loose stack of instant prints, captioned as numbered frames ("No. 01 The first reading"). Clicking the top print lifts it, swings it aside, and sets it on the back of the pile; scrolling over the stack or stepping with the arrow buttons does the same, so the pile keeps turning without leaving the page. The physics are deliberate — a small rotation per depth step, layered shadows, and a flight path that clears the pile before settling — and the whole deck is a focusable group with arrow-key control, a live frame count, and an instant, non-animated path when reduced motion is requested. No carousel chrome, no dots: the prints are the interface.

**The header is the instrument panel.** A sticky, mostly transparent rail keeps the black SafelightIO mark, section links, the manual PDF, Instagram, and the cart price within reach. Its ground is only 30% opaque, so the page reads through it until content scrolls beneath the blur; over the dark chapters the rail and every control flip to cream. The mark swaps to its cream file in safelight mode so it never disappears into the dark, and a hairline shadow appears only once the page has moved. On narrow screens the same controls move into the menu sheet with full-size touch targets.

**Discipline about facts.** Copy is anchored to the manual. Dimensions, weight, and battery runtime are explicitly not claimed. The page is a single self-contained HTML file with local images, responsive layouts, native `details` disclosures, keyboard focus styles, reduced-motion support, and accurate Product/FAQ structured data.

**Type that travels with the file.** Headlines are set in Fraunces at the 72 pt optical size; the darkroom notes and instant-print captions are Caveat. Both are embedded as base64 data URIs in the stylesheet, because browsers refuse to load local font files over `file://` and a linked `@font-face` silently falls back to Georgia. Numbers stay monospace. The handwritten line is used only where a printer would actually scrawl — a lens note, a pulled test strip, a paper reminder — never for body copy, and the manual itself is the only place a claim can come from.
