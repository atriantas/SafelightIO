01 The five tools

Five tools. One probe. No strip ladder.

Every tab works from the same sensor and the same per-paper calibration. Learn one workflow, and the rest of the darkroom arithmetic is already done.

Test strip — five exposures, one probe

01 / 05 · Exposure ← → to expose

Exposure

Meter any tone. Get the time.

Place the probe where the midtone will fall, press Measure, and read the suggested time in seconds. Set the Zone dropdown to III for a shadow or VII for a cloud and the meter scales the reading to its Zone V equivalent before it calculates.

  • Zones 0–X
  • Dodge & burn, ±2.25 stops
  • Reciprocity above 30 s

Contrast

Two readings. One grade.

Read the highlight and the shadow. SafelightIO reports the negative's ΔEV, recommends a grade, and gives one exposure time that holds both ends. When the negative exceeds the paper's range, it says so instead of flattering the result.

  • Highlight & shadow average reads
  • Grade & ISO R
  • Trims in 1/6-stop steps

Split Grade

Soft and hard, timed together.

For the negative that will not sit on one grade, the Split Grade tab calculates both legs: how much soft light, how much hard, and how long each. A split-pair slider narrows the pair when the negative needs it.

  • Soft / hard split as % of light
  • Pair from 00+5 inward
  • Total time & effective ISO R

Virtual Proof

See the print before the paper.

Walk the probe across the easel and each cell is rendered through the paper's characteristic curve — up to 40 × 30 cells. A 10-zone histogram shows where the tones sit, and cells outside the paper's range are flagged. Flip to Enlarger mode and the same scan maps your head's light uniformity.

  • Grid 2–40 × 2–30
  • Clipping flags
  • Zone V reference, auto or manual

Filter & Paper Editor

Your filters. Your numbers.

Override the exposure factor and ISO R for any variable-contrast paper and grade, and the base speed for any paper. If a paper is not calibrated, estimate its constant from ISO P using one you already trust. Add your own papers through PAPERS.CSV on the microSD card.

  • Per-paper, per-grade overrides
  • Cross-paper calibration estimate
  • Custom papers via CSV

04 Compatibility

Fits the darkroom you already have.

Twenty-two papers and six filter systems are built in. If your paper is missing, add it. If your filters have aged, correct them.

Papers

22 built in — 10 Ilford Multigrade, 12 FOMA, across RC, fibre-base, warmtone, cooltone, art, and fixed-grade stocks. Any id in PAPERS.CSV can replace a built-in entry or add a new paper.

Filter systems

6 built in — Ilford Multigrade (00–5), FOMA Variant (Y/M), and the AGFA, Kodak, Durst, and Meopta colour-head scales. Cross-brand: Ilford filters on FOMA paper, and the reverse.

Colour heads

Kodak-scale dials for Beseler, Omega/Chromega, and De Vere; AGFA, Durst, and Meopta dials are built in. Under-lens and head filter systems both work.

Fixed grade

FOMA fixed-grade papers are supported. The Contrast tab reports Fixed grade and Split Grade is not applicable — as it should be.

Light sources

Tungsten, halogen, and LED enlarger lamps. Each paper's calibration is made under your own head, so it absorbs the difference between lamps.

Your own papers

Drop a PAPERS.CSV on the microSD card: one P row per paper defines it, the F rows that follow define its filter grades. Comma or semicolon separators both work.

Where a manufacturer publishes no table — FOMA filters on Ilford papers, for example — the meter derives an estimate from the closest published data and marks it with ~ in the filter lists. You can always override the result in the Filter Editor. No black boxes.

05 Setup

Fifteen minutes, once per paper.

Calibration is one test strip you already know how to make. After that, every reading on that paper is a real time.

≈15 min · per paper ≈5 min · sensor, once
01

Choose the paper

Pick the brand and type in Settings. Remove any contrast filter — every calibration reading is made unfiltered, with the enlarger set at the stop and height you actually print with.

02

Meter, then print one strip

Put the probe on the middle gray point and press Measure. Note the lux. Make a test strip, develop it fully, and keep the section whose midtone is exactly right.

03

Enter two numbers

Type that lux and that time into Calibration Lux and Calibration Time. The constant is stored per paper. Exposure, Contrast, Split Grade, and Virtual Proof now produce real numbers.

Optional · one-time

Calibrate the sensor to your darkroom

A dark-offset sweep across all four gains and six integration times, then a two-phase light calibration under your enlarger's own lamp. About five minutes, once per device. Redo it if the darkroom temperature swings or readings disagree across gains.

07 FAQ

The objections, answered.

If yours is not here, write to Cyanowood directly. You will get a person, not a macro.

support@cyanowood.com

I'm fine with test strips. Why change?

If your loop is already tight, you may not need this. The meter earns its place when you change paper, grade, or magnification — or when you are printing fibre and every bracket costs money and a development cycle.

The point is not to replace your judgment. It is to remove the guesswork before the judgment starts: one reading instead of a ladder of strips, every time the paper, grade, or magnification changes.

€160 is a lot for a hobby.

It is a one-time tool, not a subscription: no account, no cloud, no per-print cost. It replaces a loop that spends paper every time your setup changes, and it keeps working across every paper you calibrate.

Whether that pays for itself is your arithmetic to run, not ours. If it does not earn its place, the 14-day return window is there.

Will it work with my enlarger, filters, and paper?

It works with under-lens filters and colour heads, fixed-grade and variable-contrast papers, and tungsten, halogen, or LED sources. Six filter systems are built in, including cross-brand data for Ilford and FOMA.

If your paper is not in the 22 built-ins, add it in PAPERS.CSV on the microSD card. If your aged filters print differently from the datasheet, correct the factor in the Filter Editor.

Does it work with LED or cold-light heads?

Yes. The sensor measures the light that reaches the easel, whatever makes it — tungsten, halogen, LED, or a cold-light tube. A variable-contrast LED head measures like any other source; it is the intensity that sets your time.

What can differ is how a head's filters attenuate light. Built-in factors are manufacturer averages, and your head may sit 10–30% away from them. Measure the real factor in the Filter Editor; the reading itself needs no adjustment.

How accurate is it?

Accuracy is anchored to your own print. Each paper is calibrated against one test strip that already looks right, so the constant includes your enlarger, lens, developer, and paper — not an average.

The sensor is calibrated against real lux and gets a dark-offset sweep across every gain. Published filter factors are averages; the manual notes your enlarger may differ by 10–30%, and the Filter Editor exists to correct that. Always meter without a contrast filter.

Handbuilt sounds risky.

It is built to order in Greece and supported directly by Cyanowood. Firmware updates arrive over USB-C, calibrations survive them, and your settings and papers are readable files on the card — nothing is trapped in an app.

Fourteen-day returns apply. If something is wrong, email support; that is the same address that ships the meter.

Do I need a phone or a computer?

No. SafelightIO is standalone. The microSD card is optional — it is there for settings backup, session export, and adding custom papers. Without a card, everything lives in internal flash.

Can I use it with fixed-grade papers?

Yes. Fixed-grade papers get exposure timing and Virtual Proof, and the Contrast tab reports “Fixed grade”. Split Grade is not applicable because there is no second grade to split to. FOMA fixed-grade stocks are included in the built-in database.

Why calibrate for every paper?

Every emulsion and surface turns the same light into a different density. Calibration is one test strip you already know how to make: meter a mid-tone, print the strip, then enter that lux and the time of the section that looks right. The constant is stored per paper and includes your enlarger, lens, developer, and paper.

Switch paper and the meter reloads that paper’s calibration. Until a paper is calibrated, the Exposure tab says calibrate rather than inventing a time.

How long does the first setup take?

About five minutes once per device for the sensor: a dark-offset sweep takes roughly 30 seconds at 100 ms integration and up to about three minutes at 600 ms, then a two-phase light calibration under your enlarger’s own lamp.

Then about fifteen minutes per paper: one reading, one test strip, two numbers. After that the paper is done — redo it only if the developer, lens, or your confidence in the old calibration changes.

How long does the battery last, and how do I charge it?

The manual does not publish a runtime, so we do not either. It runs from a LiPo cell charged over USB-C at the left side. The battery bar shows a load-compensated percentage and changes to Charging, Fully charged, or Battery absent.

At 10% a red charge-now warning replaces the bar. There is deliberately no auto-sleep, so the meter keeps running until you plug it in — charge it before that warning if you can.

Why does the screen flicker when I press Measure?

That is the backlight dimming to 6% for the sensor read. The LCD can contaminate a lux reading in a darkroom, so every Measure press dims the screen first, takes the reading, then restores your brightness.

Long sequences make it more visible — 16 samples at 600 ms can last around ten seconds. If it distracts you, lower the sample count or integration time in Settings. There is no setting to disable the dimming.

Does it handle reciprocity failure?

Yes, automatically above 30 seconds. Paper under-responds during long, dim exposures, so the meter lengthens the time with a Schwarzschild model — an exponent of about 0.07 for RC paper and 0.10 for fibre-base, which gives the stronger correction.

The correction is folded into Exposure, Contrast, and Split Grade totals, and the Exposure Note tells you how many seconds were added.

What if my real filter factors differ from the built-ins?

They probably do. Built-in factors are manufacturer averages, and the manual notes your enlarger, lamp, and aged filters can differ by 10–30%. The Filter Editor lets you measure a filter with a step wedge and store the real factor and ISO R — up to 64 overrides.

Cross-brand combinations without a published table are estimates, marked ~ in the lists. Your override always wins.

Can I mix filter brands and papers?

Yes. Six filter systems are built in — Ilford Multigrade, FOMA Variant, and AGFA, KODAK, DURST, and MEOPTA colour heads — and all 22 papers work with all of them. Use Ilford under-lens filters on FOMA paper, or a Durst head with Ilford paper.

Where a manufacturer publishes no table, the meter derives an estimate from the closest published data and marks it ~. For FOMATONE MG Classic, only the DURST and MEOPTA rows, grades 0–4, are datasheet values; the rest are estimates. Your measured override always wins.
