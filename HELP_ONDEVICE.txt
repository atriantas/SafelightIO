DARKROOM EXPOSURE METER -- ON-DEVICE HELP

Tabs: Exposure / Contrast / Split Grade / Virtual Proof /
      Filter Editor.
Settings: paper, filter brand, sensor, calibration,
      brightness, power.


=== FIRST-TIME SETUP - READ THIS FIRST ===

Welcome! This device is a light meter for your darkroom
enlarger. It reads the brightness of the projected image
on your easel and tells you exactly how many seconds to
expose your photographic paper. It can also recommend
which contrast filter to use, and even show you a
preview of what the print will look like.

But before it can do any of that, it needs to LEARN two
things about YOUR darkroom:

  THING 1 - PAPER CALIBRATION (required, 15 minutes)
    The meter needs to know how sensitive your specific
    paper is, with your enlarger. You teach it this 
    by making ONE test print and entering the numbers. 
    Do this once per paper type.
    See "Calibrate a New Paper" below.

  THING 2 - SENSOR SETUP (recommended, 5 minutes)
    Run Sensor Offset Calibration (in total darkness)
    and Sensor Light Calibration (under moderate
    enlarger light). These ensure the readings are
    accurate on your specific device. See "Settings"
    tab, under Sensor Offset / Sensor Light.

HOW THE METER THINKS
  The meter's core idea is simple: lux x constant =
  seconds. You measure the light on the easel in lux
  (with the sensor). The "constant" is a number unique
  to your paper + darkroom setup - you find it once by
  making a test print. After that, any new lux reading
  instantly gives you the right exposure time.
  Contrast filters, zone measurements, and everything 
  else are layers built on top of this one 
  relationship.

  Think of calibration as introducing your meter to
  your paper: "When I give you 2.5 lux of light on
  Ilford Cooltone paper, what exposure time do you
  need?" The test print answers that question. From
  then on, the meter can answer it for any lux value.

WHAT YOU NEED BEFORE YOU START
  - Your enlarger set up and focused at your working
    height and aperture (e.g. f/8 or f/11).
  - A negative in the carrier. Ideally a flat, 
    evenly lit mid-tone scene (a grey card, a plain
    wall, or an evenly exposed frame of clear sky). 
    Avoid negatives with strong contrast
    or calibration.
  - One sheet of the paper you want to calibrate.
  - Your normal developing trays/tanks, chemicals,
    and timer ready.
  - The darkroom safelight off, enlarger ready 
    to use.

CALIBRATION VS FILTER FACTORS
  Calibration sets the base sensitivity ("how fast
  is this paper?"). Filter factors describe how much
  EXTRA time each contrast filter adds. You MUST
  calibrate before the meter can work at all. You
  SHOULD measure your real filter factors for best
  accuracy, but you can start with the built-in
  FOMA and Ilford manufacturer values and refine
  later.

=== PAPERS.CSV (add your own papers) ===
With the SD card in, a PAPERS.CSV file can add papers
or fix built-in data. First boot with a card writes a
commented template - edit it on a computer and reboot.
P row = paper, F rows = its filters; comma or semicolon
separated. An id matching a built-in paper replaces
it; other ids are added at the end of the list.
Custom papers save their calibrations in SETTINGS.TXT
on the card only (no card = no custom-paper saves).

=== SETTINGS ===
=== WHERE SETTINGS ARE SAVED ===
With an SD card inserted (FAT32) everything you edit
(brightness, sensor setup, per-paper calibrations,
trims, filter overrides) is saved to SETTINGS.TXT on
the card about 1.5 s after the last edit. The sidebar
shows 'SD: settings.txt'. Without a card the device
saves to internal flash instead ('SD: none (flash)')
and the save briefly freezes the screen - normal.
The first boot with a new card copies the current
settings onto it; after that the card is the master.
A backup (SETTINGS.BAK) is kept after every save.

Sidebar Export: writes SESSION.CSV to the card - paper,
filter, calibration, the last Contrast/Split readings
and results, and every measured Virtual Proof cell
(col,row,lux,gray,clipping). Open in a spreadsheet.

Sidebar Load: restores the latest exported session
(paper, Contrast/Split readings, Virtual Proof cells;
results are recomputed, not copied). A toggle decides
whether the session's calibration is loaded too. Asks
for confirmation when current measurements exist.

Sidebar Backup/Restore: Backup writes a manual
snapshot to BACKUP.TXT and stores the same state
in the device flash (it survives any number of
later saves; the flash copy survives a lost card).
Calibrations of papers ADDED via PAPERS.CSV stay
card-only (they cannot exist without the card).
Restore loads it back after a confirmation and
reboots. Copy BACKUP.TXT between
devices to transfer your calibrations.

Brightness slider
  LCD backlight, 1 - 100. The lowest step keeps the
  panel just lit. At power-on the backlight runs at
  this lowest level (7% PWM) during boot and switches
  to your saved brightness once the UI is ready.

Battery Bar
  Red bar in the corner, updates every 5 seconds.
  States:
    Discharging:  red percentage bar (load-sag
      compensated)
    Charging:     orange "Charging..." label (USB
      plugged in)
    Full:         green "Fully charged" label
    No battery:   grey, empty bar
  Charging is detected from the battery voltage itself
  (plug/unplug load steps), so plugging in / unplugging
  is recognised within seconds.
  Low battery: at 10% the bar is replaced by a red
  "Battery low" warning. The device has
  no sleep mode -- plug in USB to charge.

Paper Brand / Paper Type
  Pick the emulsion you are printing on. Used by every
  tab. Switching paper reloads its calibration and
  trims.

Filter Brand dropdown
  Which contrast-filter system is in your enlarger
  head. Options: Ilford Multigrade (00-5), FOMA
  Variant (Y/M), AGFA, KODAK, DURST, MEOPTA.
  CROSS-BRAND COMPATIBLE: you can use Ilford filters
  on FOMA paper, FOMA filters on Ilford paper, or dial
  in a colour head with any paper. Filter data from
  manufacturer datasheets. For FOMATONE MG Classic only
  the DURST/MEOPTA dials (grades 0-4) are published; its
  other colour-head rows are derived estimates marked
  with ~. You can use the guide at Start tab to edit
  the ISO R and Lengthening Factor of each filter on
  every paper.

Calibration Lux / Calibration Time
  The lux you measured during a test print, and the
  seconds that produced the correct print at that lux.
  Both accept 0.00 - 99.99. Together they set this
  paper's calibration. Stored 1.5 s after the last
  edit. See "Calibrate a new paper" below.

Calibration Constant
  Derived from the two boxes above. Larger = darker
  paper or smaller aperture. Per-paper number stored.

Sensor Gain
  Auto / LOW (1x) / MED (25x) / HIGH (428x) / MAX (9876x).
  Auto (default): every measurement starts at MAX
  automatically and steps the gain down only if the
  reading would saturate - so dim scenes get the finest
  resolution and bright scenes step down until they fit.
  Pick a specific gain to pin the meter to it. The
  reading is then taken at that gain ONLY; if the scene
  is too bright for it the meter reports an error
  instead of stepping down. Best for very low light:
  MAX gain + 600 ms integration (Sensor Offset
  Calibration covers every gain and time in one sweep).
  At 100 ms integration the meter steps down a bit early
  (75% of full scale) to avoid clipping from lamp
  flicker.

Integration Time
  100 - 600 ms per reading. Default 200 ms. Longer =
  less noise but slower. Lower (100 ms) speeds up
  Virtual Proof scans.

Sample count
  1 - 16 readings averaged per Measure press. Default
  5. Raise for very low light or to even out lamp
  flicker.

Sensor Offset Calibration / Clear Offset
  Dark-current baseline noise calibration. Sweeps ALL
  four gains at ALL six integration times (24 slots)
  in one press, so every setting gets its own dark
  baseline. Sensor must be in real darkroom printing
  conditions, TOTAL DARKNESS when you press it
  (sensor open on easel, head off, lights and safelight
  off). Takes ~30 s at 100 ms up to ~3 min at 600 ms;
  the lux label counts up ("dark calib... 12/24").
  Persisted automatically.
  Do once, redo if drift suspected - dark roughly
  doubles every 8-10 C, so re-run after big
  temperature changes.
  A confirmation screen appears before it starts.

Sensor Light Calibration / Clear Light Cal
  Two-phase gain calibration. Phase 1: sensor under a
  STEADY light. Then the meter asks you to DIM the
  light ~4 stops (stop down or move the sensor back) -
  tap Yes to measure phase 2, or No to finish with
  phase 1 only.
  Each phase measures the real gain ratio of BOTH
  sensor channels (visible and infrared); the phases
  are chained so MAX and HIGH - the gains your dim
  readings use - get real measured factors. LOW is
  pinned at 1x (the datasheet reference gain).
  Use the enlarger's own light when possible: its
  infrared content makes the CH1 factor measurable.
  A confirmation screen appears before it starts.
  Result messages (shown on every tab's lux label):
    "gain cal set"           every stage measured
    "gain set - MAX sat"     that stage keeps its
      (or "HIGH zero")       datasheet factor; the
                             others are calibrated
    "gain cal failed -       no adjacent pair was
      adjust light"          measurable at either
                             level: nothing saved
    "... - moved"            light drifted >3% during
                             the sweep (announced only;
                             factors still applied)
    "... - no IR"            too little infrared for a
                             CH1 factor (announced only)
  A stage that saturates or averages under ~50 counts
  ("zero") is normal. Below that floor a ratio would
  be ADC quantization noise.

Lux scale update
  Lux readings now use the correct device factor for
  this sensor's glass package (TSL25911FN), so the
  readings match a real lux meter. This update
  discards settings stored by earlier firmware:
  re-run Sensor Offset / Sensor Light Calibration
  and calibrate your papers once. A clear glass
  window over the sensor dims readings a few percent
  - the paper calibration absorbs that, so no extra
  setting is needed.


=== EXPOSURE TAB ===
What it does: tells you how many seconds to expose this
paper, given the lux reading at the easel. You  can use 
this tool with fixed and variant grade papers.
Always take readings without contrast filter.

Measure
  Take an averaged lux reading right now.

Lux Number
  The most recent lux reading.

Zone dropdown (0 - X, default Zone V)
  Tells the meter which Zone System zone you are
  actually measuring. The reading is automatically
  scaled to its Zone V equivalent:
    zone_v_lux = measured_lux x 2^(5 - zone)
  The last selection is remembered across power
  cycles.
  Use this to meter shadows (Zone III), highlights
  (Zone VII), or any other tone - the calibration
  (done at Zone V) stays valid. With this feature 
  you can measure a cloud, select Zone VII and you 
  will get the exposure time to achieve Zone VII 
  at the cloud. See the user manual
  for a zone-to-subject lookup table.

Exposure Time
  The final number, in seconds. Times below 1 s or
  above 300 s get a sanity hint appended. While
  anything is missing the label tells you what to do:
   "calibrate"   - this paper has no calibration yet
   "measure lux" - calibrated, but no reading yet

Filter Grade Transform dropdown
  Transform the exposure time with the lengthening
  factor of the selected contrast filter. "No filter"
  leaves the time unchanged.

DODGE & BURN Panel
  A slider below the filter transform lets you apply
  a dodge (less time) or burn (more time) to the
  calculated exposure. The slider range is -9 to +9,
  each step is 1/4 stop (+/- 2.25 stops total).
  The slider resets to 0 when you switch paper.
  Two readouts show:
    ADDITIONAL TIME  - how many seconds the dodge/burn
                       adds or subtracts
    TOTAL TIME       - the final exposure time with
                       dodge/burn applied
  Set to 0 for no adjustment. Use this to fine-tune
  the print after evaluating the first working print.


=== CONTRAST TAB ===
What it does: spotmeter highlight + spotmeter shadow ->
recommends a single contrast grade and one exposure
time that hits both ends. You  can use this tool
with fixed and variant grade papers.
Always measure without contrast filter.

Measure Highlight / Measure Shadow
  Average-read the easel for that zone. Until both
  readings exist, the result labels show -- and the
  suggested-time label tells you which is missing.

Highlight Lux / Shadow Lux
  The two lux readings.

Delta EV Display
  The negative's contrast range, in stops. 3 stops =
  moderate. 5 stops = extreme.

Recommended Grade
  The contrast grade the meter recommends for this
  negative on the selected paper.

Match Quality
  How well that grade fits: Exact / Close / Acceptable
  / Approximate. "Acceptable" or "Approximate" means
  the negative isn't a great fit for any single grade
  -- consider Split Grade.

Analysis Reasoning
  One-line explanation. Warnings here flag a negative
  that exceeds the paper's range (too contrasty or too
  flat).

Suggested Exposure Time
  Single midpoint exposure time, in seconds, including
  the recommended grade's filter factor. Long exposures
  get automatic reciprocity-failure correction.
  The Exposure Note tells you when it was applied, and
  also shows a sanity hint for times below 1 s or above
  300 s.

Midpoint Lux
  The geometric mean of your two readings after the
  Overall-Offset slider - the brightness the time
  calculation uses.

Meter Calibration Value
  The paper's raw calibration constant. The
  Overall-Offset slider does NOT change it - the
  slider's effect is folded into the Midpoint Lux
  value instead.

Exposure Note
  Whether long-exposure reciprocity correction was
  applied and how much it added.

Overall Offset slider (+/- 1.5 stops)
  Push/pull the whole time without changing grade.

Contrast Bias slider (+/- 1.5 stops)
  Bias grade selection. Positive = harder grade.

Highlight Trim / Shadow Trim sliders (+/- 1.5 stops
each)
  Treat that zone as if it received more (+) or less
  (-) exposure on the negative. Affects both contrast
  grade and time in order to have the perfect exposure
  on shadows and highlights.

Reset button
  Zero all four sliders for the current paper.

The four sliders persist PER PAPER.
Each slider step is 1/6 stop.

DODGE & BURN Panel
  A slider below the analysis results lets you apply
  a dodge (less time) or burn (more time) to the
  suggested midpoint exposure. Range is -9 to +9,
  each step = 1/4 stop (+/- 2.25 stops total).
  Two readouts show:
    ADDITIONAL TIME  - how many seconds the dodge/burn
                       adds or subtracts from the
                       midpoint time
    TOTAL TIME       - the final midpoint exposure
                       with dodge/burn applied
  Set to 0 for no adjustment. Use this when the print
  needs an overall nudge in one direction.


=== SPLIT GRADE TAB ===
What it does: two readings (highlight/shadow) -> two
filter changes during the exposure -> a print that holds
both ends. Use this when the Contrast tab says "exceeds
paper range". You can only use this tool with variant
grade papers. Always measure without contrast filter.
Long total times get automatic reciprocity correction

Measure Highlight / Measure Shadow
  Average-read the easel for that zone. Until both
  readings exist, the result labels show -- and the
  suggested-time label tells you which is missing.

Delta EV
  Negative's contrast range, in stops.

Equivalent Grade
  The single contrast grade whose ISO(R) paper range
  matches the negative's contrast range - the grade a
  single-filter print would use. Reference only.

Analysis Reasoning
  The split, expressed as a percentage of each
  filter's UNFILTERED-EQUIVALENT LIGHT (time divided
  by the lengthening factor), the soft:hard light
  ratio, and the split's effective ISO R (e.g. "65%
  00 / 35% 5 (light soft:hard 1.9:1, ISO R 84)"),
  plus any trim, reciprocity (with the seconds
  added), or out-of-range note.

Soft Filter Name / Soft Time
  Which soft filter to drop in, and for how many
  seconds.

Hard Filter Name / Hard Time
  Which hard filter to drop in, and for how many
  seconds.

Total Time
  Soft + Hard. The total bulb-on time the print sees.
  The Analysis Reasoning shows a sanity hint for
  totals below 1 s or above 300 s.

Meter Calibration Value
  The paper's raw calibration constant. The
  Overall-Offset slider does NOT change it - the
  slider's effect is applied in the time calculation
  instead.

Overall Offset slider (+/- 1.5 stops)
  Push/pull total time, soft/hard ratio unchanged.

Contrast Bias slider (+/- 1.5 stops)
  Shifts the soft/hard split. Positive = more hard
  filter time.

Highlights slider (+/- 1.5 stops)
  Reinterprets the highlight reading, like the
  Contrast tab: + = treat the highlight as if the
  negative got MORE exposure there (prints darker);
  - = LESS exposure (prints lighter, whiter). Changes
  the split ratio and the total exposure together.

Shadows slider (+/- 1.5 stops)
  Reinterprets the shadow reading, like the Contrast
  tab: + = treat the shadow as if the negative got
  MORE exposure there (prints darker); - = LESS
  exposure (prints lighter). Changes the split ratio
  and the total exposure together.

Reset button
  Zero all four split trims and reset the filter pair
  to the extremes for the current paper.

These four sliders persist PER PAPER, separate from the
Contrast-tab trims.

Split filter pair slider
  Picks the split pair. Centre = the paper's extremes
  (00/5 or 2xY/2xM2). Slide left to step the HARD
  filter softer (00+5 -> 00+4 -> 00+3 ...); slide right
  to step the SOFT filter harder (00+5 -> 0+5 -> 1+5
  ...). On FOMA Variant the no-filter Special grade
  is included as a selectable leg. Times are recomputed
  with the correct per-grade speed compensation, and a
  pair too narrow for the negative's range gets a
  warning.

DODGE & BURN Panel
  A slider below the analysis results lets you apply
  a dodge (less time) or burn (more time) to BOTH
  the soft and hard filter times equally. Range is
  -9 to +9, each step = 1/4 stop (+/- 2.25 stops).
  Four readouts show:
    ADDITIONAL TIME  - how many seconds the dodge/burn
                       adds or subtracts
    TOTAL TIME       - the final time for each leg
                       (soft and hard shown separately)
  Set to 0 for no adjustment. The soft/hard ratio is
  preserved; both legs scale together.

Tip: if one leg dwarfs the other, the negative is
outside the paper's contrast range. Pre-flash, dodge,
or change paper.


=== VIRTUAL PROOF TAB ===
What it does: sweep the easel and the device paints a
grayscale preview of how the print will look on this
paper at this grade -- before you commit a sheet.
Always measure without contrast filter. Lower the
Integration Time and Sample count in Settings for
faster scans.

Grid Width / Grid Height
  Cells across x down. 2..40 x 2..30. More cells =
  finer preview, longer to scan. Changing size WIPES
  the grid.

Proof Grid (the canvas)
  One block per cell, painted with the predicted gray.
  Cell frame colours:
    amber      - next cell to read (cursor)
    red        - your tap (selection)
    cyan       - Zone V reference
    light gray - highlight clipping
    dark gray  - shadow clipping
    no frame   - ordinary measured cell

Measure Next Cell button
  Read one cell, advance the cursor. When Stability
  Enabled is ON, this button becomes Start/Stop Scan
  and the device walks the grid for you. Auto readings
  follow a snake (slalom) pattern.

Set Zone V Reference button
  Use the selected (red-framed) cell as the middle-gray
  anchor. Every other cell is re-derived against it.

Clear Grid button
  Erase all samples, the selection, and the reference.

Stability Enabled switch
  Turn on auto-settle detection so the scan records
  each cell only when its reading has stopped changing.

Stability Tolerance (%)
  Two consecutive reads must agree within this
  percentage to count as stable.

Stability Min Delta (lux)
  Floor on Tolerance for very dim cells.

Stability Min Stable
  Consecutive stable reads required before recording.

Stability Max Wait (ms)
  After this long without settling, record anyway.
  Keeps the scan from stalling.

Filter Grade dropdown
  The grade the preview is rendered for. Changing it
  re-renders every cell.

Histogram (proofHistogram)
  10 Zone distribution of the predicted print tones.

Selected-sample readouts (Lux / Zone / ISO R / EV Range /
Density / Exposure / Clipping)
  Detail for the red-framed cell. With NO cell selected
  the Clipping slot turns into a global verdict:
    "Negative too contrasty"
    "Negative too flat"
    "Clipping: --"
  Use this to decide if the negative fits the paper.

Cells are walked in a snake order (left->right, then
right->left on the next row) so the sensor doesn't have
to jump back. When a full scan completes, Zone V is set
automatically to the average-brightness cell.


=== ENLARGER MODE - LIGHT UNIFORMITY TEST ===
Flip the Enlarger Mode switch ON and the same grid
measures the BARE enlarger light (no negative) and
paints a uniformity map instead of a print preview.

Enlarger setup
  No negative in the carrier. Lens at your WORKING
  aperture, head at your WORKING height (falloff
  changes with magnification - test at print size).
  Let the lamp warm up a few minutes first; a cold
  lamp drifts while you measure. Put an opal/diffusing
  disc over the sensor, keep it flat on the easel, and
  keep the device from shading the point you read.

Taking a measurement
  Use a 3x3 grid (the classic nine-point test) or
  5x5. Turn Stability Enabled ON and press Start
  Scan; sweep the easel like a negative scan. When it
  finishes the reference point is set automatically.

Reading the map
  blue / light blue  - point dimmer than average
  gray               - on average
  light amber / red  - point brighter than average
  white frame        - outside the Uniformity Limit
  cyan frame         - reference point
  red frame          - your tap (selection)

Readouts (in Enlarger mode)
  Lux:    the selected point's brightness
  Dev:    % and EV above/below the average (or below
          the reference point, when one is set)
  Range:  brightest-to-dimmest spread in EV
  Mean:   average brightness of the measured points
  Min/Max: dimmest / brightest point
  Verdict: GOOD / MARGINAL / UNEVEN unif xx% +/-x%.
          unif = the classic min/max uniformity
          figure. +/-x% = the worst point's
          deviation. ";corn x%" (3x3 grids) = the
          four-corners average vs the centre.

Uniformity Limit (%)
  The tolerance (5..50 %, default 15 %) the verdict
  and white frames are judged against. GOOD = every
  point within half the limit. MARGINAL = worst point
  between half and the full limit. UNEVEN = beyond.

Interpreting results
  A healthy enlarger keeps every point within about
  +/-15 % (0.2 EV) of the centre at working aperture.
  Dark corner     -> condenser/diffuser or lamp
                     misalignment
  Centre bright, corners dark -> normal condenser
                     falloff; burn edges or stop down
  Off-centre hot spot -> lamp needs centring

Tip: measure the centre, tap it, press Set Reference,
and every other point is read relative to the centre -
the way enlarger specs are quoted. Flip the switch
back any time: both views share the same measurements.

Session Export / Load saves the mode with the cells,
so a saved uniformity run reloads as one.


=== FILTER EDITOR TAB ===
What it does: override the built-in filter factors, ISO
R values, and paper base speed (ISO P) for any paper +
grade combination. All edits persist to flash and
survive power cycles. Every other tab uses the effective
(overridden) values in its calculations. You  can use 
this tool with fixed and variant grade papers.

Filter Grade dropdown
  Pick the grade to edit. Populated from the current
  paper + filter brand combination.

Current factor / Current ISO R labels
  The EFFECTIVE values used by all tabs. Shows your
  override if one exists, else the built-in default.

Default Factor / Default ISO R labels
  The BUILT-IN factory values for reference.

Factor spinbox
  Edit the filter's exposure multiplier (e.g. 2.17
  for grade 2 on Ilford MG FB Classic).
  Two decimal places. Set to 1.00 to skip the filter
  factor entirely.

ISO R spinbox
  Edit the contrast range this grade produces. Higher
  ISO R = softer contrast. Affects grade matching in
  the Contrast and Split Grade tabs.

ISO P spinbox
  The paper's base unfiltered speed (default 500 for
  Ilford MG). Per-paper setting (not per-grade). Higher
  ISO P = shorter times; lower = longer.

Reset to defaults button
  Clear ALL overrides for the current paper and restore
  the built-in database values.

Base ISO P label
  Shows current effective ISO P for the paper.

--- Advanced calibration tools (bottom of tab) ---

Set as Ref button
  Snapshots the current paper's calibration (lux +
  time + ISO P) as a reference template for
  cross-paper estimation.

Est. Cal from ISO P button
  Estimates a calibration for the current paper from
  the reference snapshot, scaled by ISO P speed ratio
  (uses the effective Base ISO P).

Auto Estimate All button
  Bulk-estimates calibration for EVERY paper that
  isn't already calibrated, using the reference
  snapshot. Already-calibrated papers are skipped.
  A confirmation screen appears before it runs.

Clear All Calibrations button
  WARNING: Irreversibly wipes all paper calibrations.
  A confirmation screen appears first - check it
  before pressing Yes.

When to use the Filter Editor:
  - Old/faded filters whose real factor differs from
    the datasheet specification
  - Tuning paper speed for your developer / temperature
  - Dialling in a new paper not in the built-in
    database (start from a similar paper's values)
  - Cross-brand filter fine-tuning
  - At most 64 factor/ISO R overrides are stored in
    flash; edits beyond that are not persisted.

--- Fixed-grade papers ---

FOMA FOMASPEED Normal is a fixed-grade paper [FG].
For [FG] papers:
  - Factor and ISO R spinboxes are disabled (greyed)
  - Contrast tab shows "Fixed grade", trims ignored
  - Split Grade tab is not applicable
  - Only ISO P can be adjusted


=== BACKLIGHT & SENSOR ===

Backlight dimming during readings
  The LCD backlight dims to 6% automatically during
  every Measure press (all tabs) to prevent LCD light
  from contaminating the sensor. It restores afterward.
  No setting to disable -- always active. Screen may
  flicker briefly, especially during long sample
  sequences.

=== DEVICE POWER AND CHARGING ===
- Plug a USB-C cable at the left side of the device
  to power on the device with external power.
- Plug a USB-C cable at the left side of the device
  while the device is powered on to charge the battery.
- On battery, the device runs until the battery is
  drained -- the low-battery warning is your cue to
  charge. There is no sleep mode and no power button.

=== FIRMWARE UPDATE ===
How to update your device with the latest firmware:
  1. Visit spotlightio.com on any computer.
  2. Download the latest .uf2 firmware file for your
     device model (SpotlightIO Digital Darkroom Meter).
  3. Connect the device to your computer via USB-C
     while pressing with a pin the button on the top 
     of the device.
  4. The device appears as a USB drive called "RP2350"
     on your computer.
  5. Drag and drop (or copy) the .uf2 file onto the
     RP2350 drive. The device reboots automatically
     with the new firmware.
  6. All your calibrations, filter overrides, and
     settings are preserved across firmware updates.

=== CALIBRATE A NEW PAPER ===

WHY CALIBRATE?
  Every combination of paper, enlarger bulb 
  and chemicals has a different brightness.
  The meter cannot guess yours - you must measure it
  once. Calibration tells the meter: "On THIS paper,
  in THIS enlarger, X lux of light needs Y seconds 
  of exposure to produce a correct mid-tone.
  " Without calibration the meter has no reference 
  point - it's like a clock that doesn't know what 
  time it is.

WHEN TO CALIBRATE
  - Once for every new paper type you use.
  - Again if you change your enlarger bulb.
  - Again if you switch to a different developer or
    change your development time/temperature.
  - If your prints are consistently too dark or too
    light and you've ruled out other causes.

WHAT YOU ARE MAKING: a test print that answers one
specific question - "how many seconds does THIS paper
need, to turn a known amount of light into a perfect 
mid-tone?"

DETAILED STEP-BY-STEP

Step 0 - Prepare the device
  In the Settings panel (visible alongside every tab),
  select your Paper Brand and Paper Type. Also select
  the Filter Brand you use in your enlarger head.
  Set Sensor Gain to Auto (good for most setups).
  Set Integration Time to 200 ms, Sample Count to 5.

Step 1 - Set up the enlarger EXACTLY as you will print
  Insert the negative you will use for calibration
  (see "Choosing a calibration negative" below).
  Set your working lens aperture (e.g. f/8).
  Focus sharply on the easel.
  Set the head height for your typical print size.

Step 2 - Remove ALL contrast filters
  Take every filter out of the light path. No grade 00,
  no grade 5, no colour-head filtration. The enlarger
  must project plain white light. Every reading the
  meter takes must be made WITHOUT a contrast filter.
  The meter applies filter factors mathematically
  later - if you calibrate WITH a filter in, all your
  times will be wrong.

Step 3 - Measure the light on the easel
  Place the light sensor flat on the easel, exactly
  where the middle-gray part of the image falls.
  Press the Measure button on the Exposure tab.
  The Lux Number updates. Wait for it to stabilize.
  Note this number. Example: you read 2.50 lux.
  Don't move the enlarger or change any settings
  after this step.

Step 4 - Make a test strip
  Take a fresh sheet of your chosen paper. Place it on
  the easel. Make a test strip: expose the whole sheet
  for, say, 4 seconds, then cover 1/5 of it with
  opaque card, expose another 4 seconds, cover another
  1/5, and so on. You now have five strips at 4, 8,
  12, 16, and 20 seconds.

Step 5 - Develop the test strip normally
  Develop, stop, fix, and wash exactly as you always
  do. Don't change your development time or
  temperature for this test - use your standard
  process. The whole point is to calibrate for how
  you REALLY work.

Step 6 - Pick the correct strip
  After fixing, examine the strip under good light
  (turn the room lights on). Find the segment where
  the mid-tone looks RIGHT - not too dark, not too
  light, a proper middle gray that matches what you
  saw on the negative or what you want in a print.
  Note the exposure time for that segment.
  Example: the 12-second segment looks perfect.

Step 7 - Enter the numbers
  In Settings, type your measured lux into Calibration
  Lux (e.g. 2.50). Type the correct exposure time into
  Calibration Time (e.g. 12.0). The Calibration
  Constant updates automatically. It is saved to
  permanent memory 1.5 seconds after you finish typing.

Step 8 - Verify (recommended)
  Go to the Exposure tab. With the SAME negative still
  in the carrier and everything unchanged, press
  Measure. The Exposure Time should match your correct
  test strip time. If it does, calibration is good.

CHOOSING A CALIBRATION NEGATIVE
  The ideal calibration negative is:
  - FLAT (low contrast). An evenly lit grey card,
    a plain painted wall, or a frame of clear blue sky
    are all good choices.
  - MID-TONED. Not a snow scene, not a coal mine.
    Something that should print as middle gray.
  - Evenly illuminated across the area where you will
    place the sensor.

  AVOID for calibration:
  - High-contrast scenes (bright window + dark room).
  - Negatives with large shadow or highlight areas
    where you'd place the sensor.
  - Very thin (underexposed) or very dense
    (overexposed) negatives.

WHY NO FILTER DURING CALIBRATION?
  Filter factors are multipliers. If you calibrate
  with a 2x filter in place, the meter learns "X lux
  needs Y seconds WITH a 2x filter." Later, when you
  tell it to use a grade 3 filter, it multiplies by
  the grade 3 factor AGAIN - and your times will be
  off by that 2x. Always calibrate without any filter.

WHAT THE CALIBRATION CONSTANT MEANS
  The constant = Calibration Lux x Calibration Time.
  It is the lux-seconds needed for a correct mid-tone
  print. A larger constant means the paper is slower
  (needs more light) or your aperture is smaller.

RECALIBRATING
  To re-calibrate (new developer, new lens, lost
  confidence), simply overwrite the two spinboxes
  with new values. There is no need to "clear" the
  old calibration first.

CROSS-PAPER ESTIMATION (time-saving trick)
  Once you have calibrated ONE paper properly, you can
  estimate calibrations for other papers without
  making more test strips. In the Filter Editor tab:
  1. Press "Set as Ref" on your calibrated paper.
  2. Switch to a different paper in Settings.
  3. Press "Est. Cal from ISO P" - the meter scales
     the calibration by the ISO P speed ratio.
  This is APPROXIMATE. For critical work, do a real
  test-strip calibration on each paper.

=== FILTER EXTENSION FACTOR ===

WHAT IS A FILTER EXTENSION FACTOR?
  A contrast filter works by blocking some of the
  light. A grade 2 Ilford filter, for example, lets
  through only about half the light compared to no
  filter at all. The "factor" is the number you
  multiply your unfiltered exposure time by to
  compensate. If your unfiltered time is 10 s and the
  filter factor is 2.0, your filtered time is 20 s.

  The meter uses these factors automatically. When you
  select a filter grade on any tab, the suggested
  time already includes the extra exposure needed.

WHY MEASURE YOUR OWN FACTORS?
  The built-in factor values come from manufacturer
  datasheets. They are averages measured in laboratory
  conditions. Your darkroom is different:
  - Your enlarger bulb has a specific colour spectrum
    (tungsten, halogen, LED, cold-light). Filters
    block different wavelengths differently.
  - Bulbs age and shift colour. An old bulb may be
    warmer (more red/yellow), changing how filters
    behave.
  - Lens coatings absorb some wavelengths more than
    others.
  - Reflector and condenser condition affect the light.

  Real-world factors can differ by 10-30% from the
  datasheet. If your filtered prints are consistently
  slightly too light or too dark, your real filter
  factors are likely different from the built-in ones.

WHEN TO MEASURE YOUR FACTORS
  - When you first set up the meter (for best accuracy).
  - After changing your enlarger bulb.
  - After cleaning the reflector or condenser.
  - When a new lens is installed.
  - Annually (bulb output spectrum drifts with age).
  - Whenever filtered exposures are consistently off
    but unfiltered exposures are correct.

DO I HAVE TO DO THIS?
  No - you can start printing immediately using the
  built-in manufacturer factors (only FOMA and Ilford).
  Many printers get perfectly good results with the 
  defaults. But if you notice that prints with a filter 
  are consistently darker or lighter than the meter
  predicts (while unfiltered prints are fine), your
  real filter factors differ from the defaults and
  measuring them will fix the problem.

PREREQUISITES
  - The paper you are using MUST be calibrated first
    (see "Calibrate a New Paper" above). Filter factor
    measurement depends on having accurate unfiltered
    times.
  - You need a consistent development process. If your
    development time or temperature varies, you cannot
    separate filter-factor errors from development
    errors.
  - Do ALL measurements for a given paper in one
    session. Don't measure grade 2 today and grade 3
    next week - your bulb may have aged slightly.


METHOD A - Step Wedge (RECOMMENDED)

What you need:
  A step wedge (also called a step tablet) is a 
  strip of film with discrete density steps, usually 
  21 steps at 0.15 density increments. It is one of
  the most useful darkroom tools you will ever own.

How it works:
  You make two contact prints of the step wedge - one
  without a filter, one with. Because the wedge has
  known density steps, you can find which step on the
  filtered print matches the same tone as a step on
  the unfiltered print. The ratio of exposure times
  tells you the real filter factor.

Detailed procedure:
  1. Calibrate your paper without any filter 
     (see "Calibrate a New Paper"). You now have
     a reliable unfiltered exposure time from 
     the meter.

  2. Place the step wedge emulsion-to-emulsion on a
     sheet of paper. Expose at the meter's suggested
     unfiltered time. This is your REFERENCE print.

  3. Insert the filter you want to measure (e.g. grade
     2 Ilford). Place the step wedge on a fresh sheet
     of the SAME paper. Expose at: unfiltered_time x
     datasheet_factor. This is your FILTERED print.

     Example: unfiltered time = 8.0 s, datasheet says
     grade 2 factor = 2.17. So expose for 8.0 x 2.17
     ~ 17.4 s. Round to the nearest half second.

  4. Develop BOTH prints together in the same tray,
     for the same time, at the same temperature.
     They must receive IDENTICAL development.

  5. After fixing, examine both prints side by side
     under good light. Find a density step on the
     filtered print that visually matches a step on
     the unfiltered print. If the filter factor you
     used is correct, matching steps should have the
     same tone. If the filtered print is overall
     lighter, the real factor is HIGHER than the
     datasheet value (the filter blocks more light
     than expected). If darker, the factor is LOWER.

  6. To find the exact factor, you may need to make a
     second filtered print with an adjusted time.
     Factor = (filtered time that produces matching
     tones) / (unfiltered reference time).

     Example: unfiltered reference = 8.0 s. You try
     filtered at 17.5 s but it's too light. You try
     again at 19.2 s and the tones match. Real factor
     = 19.2 / 8.0 = 2.40. The datasheet said 2.17 -
     your real factor is ~10% higher.

  7. Enter the real factor in the Filter Editor tab:
     select the paper, select the grade from the
     dropdown, type the factor into the Factor
     spinbox.

  8. Repeat for each grade: 00, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
     (Or the equivalent Y/M notations for FOMA.)


METHOD B - Grey Card Visual Match 
(GOOD - NO SPECIAL EQUIPMENT NEEDED)

What you need:
  A photographic grey card (18% reflectance). These
  cost a few dollars from any photo supplier. If you
  don't have one, a plain grey painted wall or grey
  fabric can work, but a real grey card is better
  because it is spectrally neutral.

How it works:
  You make unfiltered and filtered test strips of the
  same grey card negative, then find the exposure
  times that produce the same printed grey tone.

Detailed procedure:
  1. Calibrate your paper without any filter using the
     grey card as your calibration negative. (Follow
     "Calibrate a New Paper" above, using the grey
     card negative.) The meter now knows the correct
     unfiltered time for a middle grey on this paper.

  2. Make a test strip WITHOUT any filter at the
     meter's suggested unfiltered time (the time from
     step 1). Develop fully. Mark which segment
     matches the grey card tone. This is your
     REFERENCE grey tone.

  3. Insert the filter you want to measure. Make
     another test strip, bracketing around:
     meter_suggested_unfiltered_time x
     datasheet_filter_factor.

     Example: unfiltered time = 8.0 s. Datasheet
     grade 3 factor = 2.50. Bracket at: 16, 18, 20,
     22, 24 seconds (centered around 20 s).

  4. Develop the filtered test strip identically to
     the unfiltered one.

  5. Compare the two strips side by side. Find the
     segment on the filtered strip whose grey tone
     visually matches the reference grey from step 2.

  6. Real factor = matched_filtered_time /
     unfiltered_reference_time.

     Example: unfiltered reference segment = 8.0 s.
     The 22 s segment on the filtered strip matches.
     Real factor = 22.0 / 8.0 = 2.75. Datasheet said
     2.50 - real factor is 10% higher.

  7. Enter the real factor in the Filter Editor tab.

  8. Repeat for each grade.


METHOD C - Densitometer (ACCURATE - REQUIRES A
         DENSITOMETER)

What you need:
  A photographic densitometer (transmission or
  reflection). If you don't own one, some community
  darkrooms and schools have them. You can also use a
  flatbed scanner with calibration software as a
  poor-man's densitometer.

How it works:
  The density difference between two prints tells you
  the exact exposure ratio needed to match them.
  Factor = 10 ^ (density_difference).

Detailed procedure:
  1. Print the SAME negative twice on the SAME paper:
     once with no filter, once with the filter you
     want to measure. Use the same exposure time for
     BOTH (e.g. the unfiltered time suggested by the
     meter). Develop identically.

  2. Measure the reflection density of a mid-tone
     patch on BOTH prints with the densitometer.
     Note D_unfiltered and D_filtered.

  3. Calculate delta_D = D_unfiltered - D_filtered.

     Example: D_unfiltered = 0.72, D_filtered = 0.45.
     delta_D = 0.72 - 0.45 = 0.27.

  4. Factor = 10 ^ delta_D = 10 ^ 0.27 = 1.86.

  5. Enter this factor in the Filter Editor tab.

  6. Repeat for each grade.

  Note: This method assumes both prints received the
  same exposure time. The density difference IS the
  filter's light-blocking effect expressed in log
  units.


AFTER MEASURING - ENTER THE VALUES
  Go to the Filter Editor tab. For each grade you
  measured:
  1. Select the grade from the Filter Grade dropdown.
  2. Type your measured factor into the Factor
     spinbox.
  3. The "Current factor" label updates to show your
     override. The "Default Factor" label still shows
     the built-in value for reference.
  4. Values save to permanent memory 1.5 seconds after
     the last edit.

  You do NOT need to edit the ISO R spinbox unless
  you have also measured the real contrast range of
  your filters (advanced - rarely necessary).

HOW FILTER FACTORS AFFECT EACH TAB
  Exposure tab:
    The time is multiplied by the factor of whichever
    filter you pick in the Filter Grade Transform
    dropdown.

  Contrast tab:
    The suggested time includes the recommended
    filter's factor automatically.

  Split Grade tab:
    Both soft and hard times include their respective
    filter factors.

  Virtual Proof tab:
    The per-cell Exposure readout uses the selected
    grade's filter factor. The preview densities come
    from the grade's ISO R and Dmin/Dmax effects, not
    the factor.

RECIPROCITY FAILURE AND FILTER FACTORS
  For long exposures (above ~30 seconds), photographic
  paper becomes less sensitive - it needs MORE light
  than the linear formula predicts. This is called
  reciprocity failure. The meter automatically
  corrects for this.

  Important: the reciprocity correction is applied
  AFTER the filter factor multiplication. The
  calculation order is:
    1. Base time = calibration_constant / measured_lux
    2. Filtered time = base_time x filter_factor
    3. If filtered_time > 30 s: apply reciprocity
       correction (lengthen the time further)

  If your filter factor is wrong, TWO things go wrong:
  the base filtered time is wrong, AND if that wrong
  time crosses the 30-second reciprocity threshold,
  the reciprocity correction is applied at the wrong
  point. This is why accurate filter factors matter
  most for long exposures.

WHEN TO RE-MEASURE
  - New enlarger bulb installed.
  - After cleaning the reflector or condenser.
  - New lens purchased.
  - When filtered exposures consistently drift in one
    direction (all too dark or all too light) but
    unfiltered exposures are still correct.
  - Annually - bulb colour spectrum shifts as the bulb
    ages, and filters may fade slightly with use.

=== TROUBLESHOOTING ===
"calibrate" on Exposure Time
  This paper has no stored calibration. See above.

"measure lux" on Exposure Time
  Calibrated, but no live reading. Press Measure.

"no sensor" on any lux label
  Sensor not responding. Check cable, reboot.

"error" on a lux label
  Sensor read failed (usually saturation). Drop the
  gain one step (HIGH -> MED, MAX -> HIGH).

Suggested time consistently 2x or 1/2x off
  Gain factors not calibrated for this device, or
  different aperture / head height vs calibration.
  Run Sensor Light Calibration or recalibrate paper.

Readings disagree when switching gain
  Gain-stage factors need device-specific trimming.
  Run Sensor Light Calibration.

Lux values differ from a real lux meter
  Readings match within sensor tolerance plus your
  window glass. Re-check paper calibration if unsure.

Contrast / Split tab full of --
  One of the two readings is missing, or the paper
  isn't calibrated. The label tells you which.

Every highlight cell in Virtual Proof has gray frame
  Negative range exceeds paper + filter. Pick a softer
  grade and Recompute Preview, or use Split Grade.

Backlight looks dead
  Brightness at 1 is very dim by design. Drag right.

Calibration didn't stick after power-cycle
  Edits flush 1.5 s after the last tap. If you cut
  power inside that window they are lost. Pause one
  second before powering down.

Filter Editor spinboxes show stale values
  Re-select the grade from the dropdown to refresh.

Filter Editor reset didn't take effect
  The reset only affects the CURRENT paper. Switch to
  the paper you want, then press Reset to defaults.

Battery bar replaced by "Battery low"
  Battery reached ~10%. Plug in USB-C to charge. The
  device has no sleep mode -- the warning stays visible
  until the battery recovers.

Battery bar shows empty but device still runs
  Voltage under load may read 0% while the cell still
  has capacity. The low-battery warning appears at 10%
  as your cue. Charge the battery.

"Charging..." never goes away
  Charging is inferred from the battery voltage, not
  sensed directly. "Fully charged" appears only once the
  cell reaches the charger's top voltage; some chargers
  stop a little early, so the label may stay at
  "Charging..." even when full. Normal. Battery is
  protected; unplug when done printing.

Screen flickers briefly during Measure
  Normal. Backlight dims to 6% during sensor reads to
  prevent LCD from contaminating lux readings.

Filter Editor Factor / ISO R spinboxes greyed out
  You have a fixed-grade paper selected (FOMA
  FOMASPEED Normal). These cannot be changed.

"No reference" when pressing Est. Cal from ISO P
  Press "Set as Ref" on a calibrated paper first.

"Clear All Calibrations" wiped everything
  You pressed Yes on the confirmation screen.
  Re-calibrate your main paper, set as reference,
  then use Auto Estimate All to restore approximate
  calibrations for others.

Split Grade tab not applicable for [FG] paper
  Fixed-grade papers have single built-in contrast.
  Use the Contrast tab instead.
